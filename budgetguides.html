Budget Guides

Find the spending personality that sounds most like you, then try the budgeting strategy that fits it.

Personality Archetypes

Most people are a mix, so do not worry if you don't fit neatly into one category. Pick the one that resonates with you the most.

Saver

Keeps money aside first and spends what is left. Feels uneasy when the savings number goes down.

You might be a Saver if you:

  • Check your balance often
  • Think twice before buying anything non-essential
  • Sometimes skip things you could actually afford

Watch out for:

  • Missing out on experiences because you are too focused on saving
  • Feeling guilty about spending money
  • Not having fun with your money

Try: Pay Yourself First, with a small monthly "fun money" allocation.

Spender

Enjoys spending and treats money as something to use. Savings tend to be whatever is left, which is often nothing.

You might be a Spender if you:

  • Run out of allowance before the next one arrives
  • Treat yourself often
  • Rarely track where your money went

Watch out for:

  • Having no emergency money
  • Small daily purchases adding up

Try: The 50/30/20 Rule, or the Envelope Method to cap spending

Planner

Likes lists, schedules, and knowing what money is for before it is spent. Usually has a goal in mind.

You might be a Planner if you:

  • Have a budget and stick to it
  • Plan for big purchases
  • Feel stressed when a plan goes off track

Watch out for:

  • Spending too much time on the plan itself
  • Not leaving room for surprise expenses

Try: Zero-Based Budgeting by assigning every dollar a job, plus a small buffer for unexpected costs.

Impulse Buyer

Buys in the moment, usually because of a sale, a trend, or a bad day. Regret tends to show up later.

You might be an Impulse Buyer if you:

  • Add to cart first and think later
  • Get tempted by flash sales and online shopping
  • Have things you bought but barely use

Try: Set a 24-hour waiting period before making non-essential purchases.

Summary
